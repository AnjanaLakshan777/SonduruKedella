# Database Guidelines

## Database
PostgreSQL is the system of record for business and financial data. Local development uses Docker Compose with a named persistent volume.

## Schema Management
Use Flyway versioned SQL migrations. Do not use Hibernate schema auto-update as the production schema management strategy. Do not edit an already-applied migration in shared or persistent environments; create a new migration instead.

## Monetary Data
Use Java BigDecimal and suitable PostgreSQL NUMERIC types. Define scale and rounding policies deliberately. Never use floating-point types for money calculations.

## Core Business Areas
- Clients and contact details.
- Projects, contract values, deadlines, and payment schedules.
- Revenue and client receipts.
- Project expenses and company-level expenses.
- Commission recipients, confirmed commission terms, and payments.
- Suppliers, invoices, and partial payments.
- Materials, stock movements, and project issues.
- Employees, attendance, overtime, advances, and weekly payroll.
- Machinery rental expenses with machine and usage period.
- Document metadata and audit history.

## Financial Integrity Rules
1. Revenue records and receipts are separate concepts.
2. A client receipt must not be counted as new revenue repeatedly.
3. Supplier purchases and supplier payments are separate records.
4. Commission is calculated from the agreed contract value using the confirmed percentage and recorded calculation basis.
5. Multiple commission recipients must be supported without silently exceeding the configured allocation policy.
6. Delay deductions must not overwrite the original contract value.
7. Written-off receivables are not payments received.
8. Payroll advances must not be counted twice when deducted from weekly payroll.
9. Inventory acquisition and project stock issue must not double-count expense.
10. Project expenses must remain distinguishable from company-level expenses.

## Data Integrity
Use foreign keys, NOT NULL constraints, unique constraints, CHECK constraints, and indexes where appropriate. Use transactions for multi-record financial operations. Use optimistic locking or other suitable concurrency protection where simultaneous updates could cause incorrect balances.

## Deletion and Audit
Avoid hard deletion of finalized financial records. Use controlled correction, reversal, cancellation, or write-off workflows with a reason and audit history where appropriate.

## Development Safety
Never run destructive database resets against a database containing data that must be preserved. Back up data before risky schema operations. Verify migrations against the actual PostgreSQL version and schema.

# Proposed Schema (v2, owner decisions applied 2026-10-24)

Status: design only. No migrations or entities exist yet.

Scope: the schema supports what the existing frontend does (`frontend/src/data/types.ts`, `store/store.ts`, the forms and `lib/finance.ts`), the inventory return action the owner has approved, and the business rules the owner has agreed. Anything else the UI does not offer is left out. The frontend changes these decisions need are listed under [Frontend alignment](#frontend-alignment).

## Conventions

- Tables are plural `snake_case`. Columns are `snake_case`. Fixed enumerations are `VARCHAR` with a `CHECK` constraint, mapped to Java enums with `EnumType.STRING`. Lists the owner can edit in Settings are lookup tables.
- Primary keys: `id BIGINT GENERATED BY DEFAULT AS IDENTITY`.
- Types:
  - Money: `NUMERIC(15,2)`, rounded `HALF_UP` to 2 decimals.
  - Percentages: `NUMERIC(7,4)`, where `20.0000` means 20%.
  - Quantities: `NUMERIC(14,3)`.
  - Unit costs: `NUMERIC(15,4)`.
  - Hours: `NUMERIC(4,2)`.
  - Dates: `DATE`.
  - Instants: `TIMESTAMPTZ`.
- Every business table has `created_at`, `created_by` and `updated_at`. Tables whose rows can change also have `version BIGINT` for optimistic locking.
- **Payments** (client, other income, expense, supplier, commission, payroll) and **salary advances** follow the same rules:
  - Columns: `payment_date`, `amount > 0`, `payment_method_id`, `reference`, `notes`, `request_id UUID UNIQUE`.
  - Each one settles exactly one obligation.
  - The service locks the parent row and rejects any amount above the remaining balance. This matches the UI's "More than the remaining…" check.
  - Payments are never deleted. A correction voids the row (`voided_at`, `void_reason`), and voided rows are left out of every balance.
- Index every foreign key, plus the date columns that reports filter on.
- Balances are calculated, never stored. That covers outstanding, overdue, advance balances and payroll figures. There are two deliberate exceptions:
  - the running stock balance on each stock movement, which records the weighted average as it changes
  - the frozen payroll snapshot

  Both are described below.

## Settings and shared tables

- **commission_policy**: a single row.
  - `design_commission_pool_pct` (default 20). The company share is shown as 100 minus the pool.
  - `construction_warning_pct` (nullable).
  - Both values only trigger warnings.
- **wage_settings**: a single row with `default_daily_wage`, `hours_per_day` (default 8), `overtime_multiplier` (default 1.5), `payday` (`FRIDAY` | `SATURDAY`). The payroll week starts on Monday.
- **expense_categories**:
  - Columns: `expense_group` (`DESIGN` | `CONSTRUCTION` | `CONSULTATION` | `COMPANY`), `name`, `locked`, `system_generated`, `sort_order`.
  - Unique (`expense_group`, `name`).
  - `Materials`, `Labour` and `Commission` are `locked`. `Labour` and `Commission` are also `system_generated`, so a manual expense cannot use them.
- **Revenue categories** are fixed and have no table: Design, Construction, Consultation and Other Income. The category comes from `projects.project_type`, and `other_income` is always Other Income. Settings shows the list read-only.
- **units** and **payment_methods**: editable lists (`name` unique, `active`). Seed payment methods: Cash, Bank Transfer, Cheque, Online Payment.
- **app_users**: `username` (unique), `display_name`, `password_hash`, `enabled`. This is the minimum Spring Security needs. The UI has no user management yet.
- **activity_log**: `project_id` (nullable), `activity_date`, `kind` (`PAYMENT` | `EXPENSE` | `DOCUMENT` | `STATUS` | `COMMISSION` | `NOTE` | `WRITEOFF` | `DEDUCTION`), `message`. It feeds the project activity feed.

## Clients and projects

- **clients**: `name`, `client_type` (`INDIVIDUAL` | `BUSINESS`), `phone`, `email`, `address`, `city`, `notes`.
- **projects**:
  - Columns:
    - `code` (unique), `name`, `client_id`
    - `project_type` (`DESIGN` | `CONSTRUCTION` | `CONSULTATION`)
    - `location`, `description`
    - `contract_value`, `start_date`, `expected_completion`, `actual_completion`
    - `status` (`PLANNING` | `IN_PROGRESS` | `ON_HOLD` | `COMPLETED` | `CANCELLED`)
    - `notes`, `version`
  - Checks:
    - `contract_value > 0`, except for consultation projects, where the agreed fee is optional and may be 0.
  - Rules:
    - The contract value and the project type cannot be changed after the project is created. The UI only lets you change the status.
    - Setting the status to `COMPLETED` fills in `actual_completion` if it is empty.
  - A second unique key, (`id`, `project_type`), lets child tables restrict a feature to certain project types through composite foreign keys.
- **project_agreements**: at most one current agreement PDF per project.
  - Columns: `project_id`, `original_filename`, `content_type` (check `application/pdf`), `size_bytes`, `sha256`, `storage_key` (unique), `uploaded_at`, `removed_at`.
  - Unique `project_id` where `removed_at IS NULL`.

## Revenue and receivables

- **installments**:
  - Columns:
    - `project_id`, `description`, `due_date`, `amount > 0`
    - `kind` (`SCHEDULED` | `VARIATION`)
    - `billed_on` (nullable; null means not yet billed)
    - `version`
  - Schedule rules (enforced by the service):
    - Scheduled installments add up to `contract_value`. After variations, the whole schedule adds up to `contract_value + Σ variations`.
    - An installment that has been billed or has payments cannot be edited or removed. This matches the locked rows in the schedule editor.
  - Variations:
    - "Additional work billed" adds a `VARIATION` installment that is billed immediately.
    - The contract value does not change.
- **client_payments**: `installment_id` plus the common payment columns. A payment is allowed only when `billed_on` is set and a balance remains.
- **receivable_write_offs**: `installment_id`, `write_off_date`, `amount > 0`, `reason` (not null). Allowed only on billed installments that still have a balance. A write-off is never a payment.
- **delay_deduction_terms**: one row per construction project. It holds both the agreed terms and the deduction that was applied.
  - Columns for the terms:
    - `project_id` (unique), `project_type` (check `CONSTRUCTION`, composite FK)
    - `enabled`, `contract_deadline`
    - `rule` (`PER_DAY` | `PERCENT_PER_WEEK` | `FIXED_AMOUNT`), `rate_per_day`, `percent_per_week`, `fixed_amount`
    - `grace_days ≥ 0`, `max_deduction`
  - Columns for the applied deduction: `actual_completion`, `applied_amount`, `applied_on`, `applied_installment_id`, `notes`, `version`.
  - Checks:
    - The value column that matches `rule` is required.
    - The applied columns are either all set or all null.
  - Rules:
    - The applied amount cannot exceed the target installment's amount.
    - Clearing the applied columns reverses the deduction, as the UI's reverse action does. The activity log records both events.
- **other_income**: `income_date`, `description`, `payer`, `amount > 0`, `due_date`, `reference`, `notes`. It is not linked to a project.
- **other_income_receipts**: `other_income_id` plus the common payment columns.

## Commission

- **commission_arrangements**:
  - Columns:
    - `project_id`, `project_type` (check `IN ('DESIGN','CONSTRUCTION')`, composite FK, so consultation projects cannot have commission)
    - `recipient_name`, `role`
    - `rate_pct` (`> 0 AND < 100`)
    - `status` (`PENDING` | `CONFIRMED`), `confirmed_on`, `policy_exception`
    - `version`
  - Amount = `round(contract_value × rate_pct / 100)`. Because the contract value cannot change, the amount stays fixed once the arrangement is confirmed.
  - If the total of a project's rates goes above the design pool or the construction warning level, the service returns a warning and changes nothing. The owner may still confirm, and `policy_exception = true` records that.
- **commission_payments**: `arrangement_id` plus the common payment columns.

## Expenses

- **expenses**: manual expenses.
  - Columns:
    - Core: `expense_date`, `scope` (`PROJECT` | `COMPANY`), `project_id`, `category_id`, `description`, `payee`, `supplier_id` (nullable), `amount > 0`, `due_date`, `invoice_no`, `notes`.
    - Materials: `quantity`, `unit_id`, `unit_price`.
    - Machinery rental: `machine_type`, `usage_from`, `usage_to`.
    - Electricity: `billing_period`.
    - Attachment: `attachment_storage_key`, `attachment_filename`, `attachment_size`.
    - `version`.
  - Checks:
    - `project_id` is set exactly when `scope = 'PROJECT'`.
    - `usage_to ≥ usage_from`.
    - For materials, `amount = round(quantity × unit_price, 2)`.
  - Rules (enforced by the service):
    - The category's group must match the expense: the project's type for project expenses, `COMPANY` for company expenses.
    - `system_generated` categories are rejected.
- **expense_payments**: `expense_id` plus the common payment columns. The expense form's "paid in full / part / unpaid" choice creates zero or one payment in the same transaction.

Machinery rental is a manual expense in the `Machinery Rental` category, recording the provider, machine type, usage period and project, with payments recorded against the expense. There is no separate rental module.

## Suppliers and inventory

- **suppliers**: `name`, `contact_person`, `phone`, `email`, `address`, `supplies`, `credit_days`.
- **materials**:
  - Columns: `sku` (unique), `name`, `category`, `unit_id`, `unit_cost`, `version`.
  - `unit_cost` holds the current weighted average cost. It is updated in the same transaction as each movement and always equals the latest movement's `value_after / quantity_after`.
    - The "Default unit cost" entered when a material is created is used only until the first receipt.
    - When stock reaches zero, the last average is kept.
  - There is no reorder level and no low-stock tracking.
- **supplier_invoices**:
  - Columns:
    - `supplier_id`, `invoice_no`, `invoice_date`, `due_date`
    - `allocation` (`STOCK` | `PROJECT`), `project_id`
    - `total_amount`, `notes`
    - `attachment_storage_key`, `attachment_filename`, `attachment_size`
    - `version`
  - Unique (`supplier_id`, `invoice_no`).
  - Checks:
    - `due_date ≥ invoice_date`.
    - `project_id` is set exactly when `allocation = 'PROJECT'`, and the project must be a construction project.
  - `due_date` defaults to `invoice_date + supplier.credit_days`.
  - `total_amount` equals the sum of the lines. The service checks this.
- **supplier_invoice_lines**:
  - Columns: `invoice_id`, `line_no`, `material_id`, `description`, `quantity > 0`, `unit_id`, `unit_price > 0`, `line_amount`.
  - On a `STOCK` invoice, every line must have a `material_id`.
- **supplier_payments**: `invoice_id` plus the common payment columns. A full payment is one row and partial payments are several rows. A payment entered on the invoice form is created in the same transaction.
- **stock_movements**: an append-only ledger.
  - Columns:
    - `material_id`, `movement_date`
    - `movement_type` (`RECEIVED` | `ISSUED` | `RETURNED` | `ADJUSTMENT`)
    - `quantity_change` (signed), `unit_cost`, `total_cost` (signed, set by the service)
    - `quantity_after`, `value_after` (the material's running balance after this movement)
    - `project_id`, `supplier_invoice_line_id`, `returned_from_movement_id`
    - `reason`, `reference`
  - Checks:
    - `RECEIVED` and `RETURNED` are positive. `ISSUED` is negative. `ADJUSTMENT` is non-zero and needs a `reason`.
    - `project_id` is set exactly when the type is `ISSUED` or `RETURNED`.
    - `returned_from_movement_id` is set exactly when the type is `RETURNED`.
    - `quantity_after ≥ 0` and `value_after ≥ 0`.
  - Returns:
    - A unique key on (`id`, `material_id`, `project_id`) lets a composite foreign key (`returned_from_movement_id`, `material_id`, `project_id`) force every return to point at an issue of the same material to the same project.
    - The service also checks that the referenced row is an `ISSUED` movement.
    - Materials that reached a project through a `PROJECT` supplier invoice or a manual `Materials` expense were never issued from stock, so they cannot be returned.
  - Ledger order:
    - Each material's ledger is ordered by `id`, which is the posting order. `movement_date` is the business date.
    - The weighted average is never recalculated retroactively, so a late-entered receipt affects only movements posted after it.
    - Index: (`material_id`, `id`), plus (`project_id`, `material_id`) for project usage.
  - Where receipts come from:
    - A posted `STOCK` invoice creates one `RECEIVED` movement for each line.
    - A `RECEIVED` movement without an invoice line comes from "Record stock received", for example a cash purchase or opening stock, and creates no payable.
  - Rows are never updated or deleted. Mistakes are corrected with an `ADJUSTMENT`, or a `RETURNED` movement for an issue.

## Payroll

- **employees**: `name`, `role`, `phone`, `address`, `daily_wage > 0`, `overtime_rate > 0`, `status` (`ACTIVE` | `INACTIVE`), `joined_on`.
- **attendance**:
  - Columns: `employee_id`, `work_date`, `status` (`PRESENT` | `HALF_DAY` | `ABSENT`), `project_id` (construction project), `notes`.
  - Unique (`employee_id`, `work_date`).
- **overtime_entries**:
  - Columns: `employee_id`, `work_date`, `project_id`, `hours` (`> 0 AND ≤ 8`), `hourly_rate > 0`, `approved`, `recorded_by`, `notes`.
  - `hourly_rate` defaults to the employee's `overtime_rate`, and the person entering it may change it.
- **salary_advances**: `employee_id` plus the common payment columns.
- **payroll_adjustments**:
  - Columns: `employee_id`, `week_start`, `advance_deduction`, `other_deduction`, `other_note`.
  - Unique (`employee_id`, `week_start`).
  - These are the owner's overrides for one week.
- **payroll_lines**: a frozen snapshot of one employee's week. It is written when the first payment for that employee and week is recorded, and it is never updated afterwards.
  - Columns:
    - `employee_id`, `week_start`, `week_end`
    - `present_days`, `half_days`, `payable_days`, `daily_wage`, `base_pay`
    - `overtime_hours`, `overtime_pay`, `gross_pay`
    - `advance_deduction`, `other_deduction`, `other_note`, `net_pay`
  - Unique (`employee_id`, `week_start`).
  - Checks: `gross_pay = base_pay + overtime_pay`, `net_pay = gross_pay − advance_deduction − other_deduction`, and `net_pay ≥ 0`.
- **payroll_line_costs**: `payroll_line_id`, `project_id` (nullable), `amount`. This splits `gross_pay` across projects for profitability.
- **advance_recoveries**: `advance_id`, `payroll_line_id`, `amount > 0`. It records which advances a week's deduction recovered, oldest first.
- **payroll_corrections**: an explicit change to a frozen week.
  - Columns: `payroll_line_id`, `correction_date`, `wage_delta` (≠ 0), `project_id` (nullable), `reason` (not null).
  - It changes only the wages owed. It never changes `advance_deduction` or `advance_recoveries`, so advances cannot be counted twice.
- **payroll_payments**: `payroll_line_id` plus the common payment columns.
  - Payment is allowed once the week has ended. The UI offers per-worker payments and "Pay remaining".
  - The total paid can never exceed `net_pay + Σ wage_delta`.

**Freezing a paid week**
- Once a `payroll_lines` row exists for an employee and week, the following can no longer be changed:
  - that employee's `attendance` and `overtime_entries` for the week
  - the week's `payroll_adjustments`
  - the line and its cost and recovery rows
- The service rejects such edits.
- Any later correction is entered as a `payroll_corrections` row, with a reason.
- If a negative correction leaves the worker overpaid, the overpayment is recovered as an `other_deduction` in a later week that has not been frozen. That week's `other_note` refers to the correction.

## Calculations

These follow `lib/finance.ts` unless noted.

**Installments and receivables**
- Net amount = `amount − applied deduction` (only for the installment the deduction is applied to).
- Remaining = `net − Σ payments − Σ write-offs`.
- Status:
  - `UPCOMING`: not billed.
  - `PAID`: remaining is 0 and nothing was written off.
  - `WRITTEN_OFF`: remaining is 0 after a write-off.
  - `OVERDUE`: billed, remaining > 0 and `due_date < today`.
  - `PARTIALLY_PAID`: billed, remaining > 0, not yet due, with some payment.
  - `DUE`: billed, remaining > 0, not yet due, with no payment.
- Receivables count billed installments only. Outstanding, the overdue part of it, and amounts written off are reported as three separate figures.

**Revenue and received are separate figures**
- Revenue = `Σ net of billed installments` (dated `billed_on`) + other income (dated `income_date`).
- Received = `Σ client payments + Σ other income receipts`.
- "Scheduled, not billed" = the net of unbilled installments. It is shown as expected revenue and is not counted as revenue.

**Delay deduction**
- `days_late = max(0, completion − contract_deadline)`
- `chargeable = max(0, days_late − grace_days)`
- The amount by rule:
  - `PER_DAY`: `chargeable × rate_per_day`
  - `PERCENT_PER_WEEK`: `floor(chargeable / 7) × percent_per_week% × contract_value`
  - `FIXED_AMOUNT`: `fixed_amount` if `chargeable > 0`
- The result is capped at `max_deduction`. The owner may apply a different final amount.

**Stock costing (weighted average)**

Each movement is posted with the `materials` row locked. Let `Q` and `V` be the previous movement's `quantity_after` and `value_after`, or 0 for the first movement. Then:

| Movement | `unit_cost` | `total_cost` | New average |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `RECEIVED` | the purchase price | `round(q × price, 2)` | `(V + total_cost) / (Q + q)` |
| `RETURNED` | the referenced issue's `unit_cost` | `round(q × issue unit_cost, 2)` | `(V + total_cost) / (Q + q)` |
| `ISSUED` | the current average (`V / Q`) | `−round(q × average, 2)` | unchanged |
| `ADJUSTMENT` | the current average (`V / Q`) | `±round(q × average, 2)` | unchanged |

- After every movement:
  - `quantity_after = Q + quantity_change`
  - `value_after = V + total_cost`
  - `materials.unit_cost = value_after / quantity_after` (4 decimal places). When `quantity_after = 0`, the previous average is kept.
- Rounding: a movement that takes stock to exactly zero takes `total_cost = −V`, so no rounding residue is left in stock. A return that completes the return of an issue takes the issue's remaining cost.
- The service rejects:
  - issues or negative adjustments larger than `Q`
  - returns larger than the quantity still un-returned on the referenced issue
- Project material usage (quantity and cost) = `−Σ` of the project's `ISSUED` and `RETURNED` movements.
- Stock on hand and stock value = the latest `quantity_after` and `value_after`. These always equal `Σ quantity_change` and `Σ total_cost`, and a reconciliation query and tests check that they do.

**Project costs**: each cost has exactly one source.

| Cost | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Materials | `PROJECT` supplier invoices + `ISSUED` minus `RETURNED` movements + manual `Materials` expenses |
| Labour | attendance (`daily_wage` per present day, half for a half day) and approved overtime (`hours × hourly_rate`) on the project. Frozen weeks use `payroll_line_costs` plus `payroll_corrections`. |
| Commission | confirmed arrangements × contract value |
| Other | manual project expenses in every other category, including machinery rental |

**Profitability**
- Profit = revenue − write-offs − project costs.
- Margin = profit ÷ revenue.
- It is shown as *Estimated* until the project is `COMPLETED`, then as *Recorded*.

**Payables**
- Supplier invoice outstanding = `total − Σ payments`.
- Expense outstanding = `amount − Σ payments`.
- Commission outstanding = `amount − Σ payments`.
- An invoice or expense is overdue when something is still outstanding and `due_date < today`.

**Weekly payroll**
- `payable_days = present + 0.5 × half_days`
- `base_pay = payable_days × daily_wage`
- `overtime_pay = Σ approved hours × hourly_rate`
- `gross = base_pay + overtime_pay`
- Advance deduction = the adjustment override, or else all outstanding advances given up to payday, but never more than gross.
- `net = gross − advance deduction − other deduction`
- Remaining = `net + Σ wage_delta − Σ payroll payments`. Corrections only exist on frozen weeks.

## Preventing double counting

- **Purchasing stock is not a project cost.** A `STOCK` invoice creates a payable and `RECEIVED` movements. Cost reaches a project only through `ISSUED` movements, and `RETURNED` movements reverse it. A `PROJECT` invoice is a project cost directly and creates no stock movements.
- **Each manual purchase has one record.** Credit purchases from suppliers are entered as supplier invoices. A manual `Materials` expense is for cash purchases that are not entered as invoices. The service warns when an expense's supplier and invoice number match an existing supplier invoice.
- **Supplier payments change only the payable,** never cost.
- **Labour is counted once.** The labour cost is gross wages, taken from attendance and overtime. A salary advance is cash paid ahead of payday that `advance_recoveries` later recovers. It is not an expense and not a second payment. The worker's total received is the sum of advances and payroll payments, which equals net pay plus the recovered advances. Corrections to a frozen week change only wages, never advance recoveries.
- **Commission and labour costs come only from their own modules.** Their categories are system-generated and cannot be used for a manual expense.
- **Revenue is recognised once, at billing.** Payments settle it. Write-offs and delay deductions are separate reductions. Variations are separate installments, and the contract value never changes.
- **Payments cannot be recorded twice.** Each one is tied to a single obligation, has a unique `request_id` and is capped at the remaining balance.

## Examples

**Weighted average stock, issue and return**
1. A `STOCK` invoice for 100 bags of cement at 2,400.
   - Payable: 240,000. Stock: 100 bags, 240,000. Average: 2,400. Project cost: 0.
2. A second `STOCK` invoice for 50 bags at 2,550.
   - Payable: +127,500. Stock: 150 bags, 367,500. Average: 2,450.
3. Issue 60 bags to SK-2026-014 at 2,450.
   - Project cost: 147,000. Stock: 90 bags, 220,500. Average: still 2,450.
4. Return 5 bags against that issue, at 2,450.
   - Project cost: 134,750. Stock: 95 bags, 232,750.
5. Pay both invoices, in one or several payments each.
   - Payables: 0. Project cost: still 134,750.

The purchases never appear as project cost.

**Billing, payments and delay deduction**
- The final installment is 2,000,000 and is billed, so revenue is 2,000,000.
- Delivery is 13 days late with 3 grace days: 10 chargeable days × 15,000 = a 150,000 deduction, applied to that installment.
- Net amount and revenue: 1,850,000.
- Payments of 1,000,000 and 500,000 leave 350,000 remaining. It is overdue once the due date passes.
- The contract value does not change.

**Design commission**
- Contract value: 1,500,000. Recipients: 12% and 8% = 20%, so 180,000 and 120,000, whatever has been collected so far.
- Adding 2% more would trigger the pool warning. The owner may still confirm it as an exception.

**Payroll with an advance**
- 5 full days + 1 half day at 3,500 = 19,250. Approved overtime of 4 h × 656.25 = 2,625. Gross: 21,875.
- A 5,000 advance is deducted, so net = 16,875.
- Labour cost: 21,875. Paid to the worker: 5,000 + 16,875 = 21,875.

The advance is not counted a second time.

**Correction to a paid week**
- After the week above is paid, a missed half day is found.
- A `payroll_corrections` row of +1,750 (with a reason and the project) is added.
- Labour cost: 23,625. Remaining owed: 1,750.
- The frozen line, the attendance rows and the 5,000 advance recovery do not change.

## Frontend alignment

The schema was compared with the frontend types, forms and calculations.

**Areas where the schema and the frontend match**
- Clients, projects (agreement metadata included), installments and billing, variations, client payments, write-offs, delay deduction terms (apply and reverse), other income and receipts.
- Commissions (draft or confirmed, with warning-only policy checks), manual expenses with their category-specific fields and payments, suppliers, supplier invoices and payments.
- Stock received, issued and adjusted; employees, attendance, overtime approval, advances, payroll adjustments and payments; settings lists; the activity feed; all six reports.

**Differences from the v1 draft, changed to follow the UI**
- Retention money has been removed. The UI explicitly separates delay deductions from retention and has no retention feature.
- The machinery rental tables have been removed. In the UI, a rental is an expense.
- Removed because the UI does not use them: service lines on invoices, invoice lines split between destinations, a separate table of commission recipients, effective-dated wage rates, finalized payroll periods, a project link on other income, and reference codes for clients, suppliers and employees.
- A policy breach on commission is now a warning that the owner can override, instead of a rejection.
- Payment methods are now a lookup table that matches the editable Settings list.

**Owner decisions (2026-10-24)**
1. **Material returns:** each return is linked to the originating project and its stock issue. The stock goes back to company stock, and the project's material cost falls by the original issue cost of the returned quantity.
2. **Low stock:** removed. There is no reorder level, no low-stock status and no alerts.
3. **Stock costing:** weighted average, held in the movement ledger (see [Stock costing](#calculations)).
4. **Paid payroll weeks:** a week is frozen for an employee at that employee's first payment for it. Later changes are made through `payroll_corrections`.
5. **Revenue categories:** fixed to the four categories and shown read-only in Settings.
